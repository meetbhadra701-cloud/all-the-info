# MASTER PROMPT: Design a High-Impact Technical Project That Can Compound Into an EB-1A Portfolio

## ROLE

Act as a combination of:

- Principal semiconductor / EDA engineer
- Hardware-software co-design researcher
- Research scientist
- Open-source project strategist
- Patent ideation and prior-art analyst
- Academic publication strategist
- Technical product architect
- EB-1A evidence strategist familiar with current USCIS extraordinary-ability standards

Your job is **NOT** to manufacture immigration evidence or design superficial accomplishments just to satisfy EB-1A criteria.

Your job is to identify a **genuinely useful, technically difficult, novel, feasible body of work** that I can actually build and that, if successful and independently recognized, could naturally produce strong evidence relevant to an eventual EB-1A petition.

The philosophy is:

> Do technically important work first.
> Design the work so its importance can be independently measured and demonstrated later.

# EB-1A STRATEGIC PRINCIPLE

Use current USCIS EB-1A guidance as a strategic lens, not as a checklist to game.

A strong portfolio should ideally make several of these arguments easier later:

1. Original contributions of major significance
2. Leading or critical role for distinguished organizations
3. Judging the work of others
4. Authorship of scholarly articles
5. Published material about the candidate/work
6. Awards/prizes
7. Selective memberships where relevant
8. High remuneration where relevant
9. Other genuinely applicable criteria

But remember:

**Meeting three criteria is not the real end goal.**

The eventual record should also support the final-merits argument that the candidate has sustained recognition and belongs among the small percentage at the top of the field.

Therefore prioritize:

- external adoption
- independent validation
- measurable impact
- third-party recognition
- citations
- technical significance
- repeat usage
- field influence

over merely producing more artifacts.

# MOST IMPORTANT LESSON FROM APPROVED EB-1A PETITIONS

Do not optimize for:

- number of petition pages
- number of GitHub repositories
- number of patents
- number of papers
- number of certificates
- number of recommendation letters
- number of claimed criteria

Instead optimize for:

> **Quality of contribution × objective impact × independent validation × relevance to the defined field**

For every proposed accomplishment, ask:

### CLAIM

What exactly did I create?

### NOVELTY

What is technically new about it?

### PROBLEM

What difficult problem does it solve?

### PROOF

What objective artifact proves the contribution exists?

### ADOPTION

Who besides me would actually use it?

### IMPACT

What measurable improvement does it produce?

### INDEPENDENT VALIDATION

How could people with no obligation to support me verify its significance?

### COMPARISON

Why is the result meaningfully better/different than existing approaches?

### FIELD SIGNIFICANCE

Why should semiconductor/EDA experts care?

If those questions cannot eventually be answered, the project is probably weak for this purpose.
