Every serious candidate must survive ALL of these before independent Claude/Codex review.

---

## TEST 1 — DOMAIN-STRIPPED REDUCTION

Remove the semiconductor vocabulary.

Ask what remains.

Example:

> “Automatically synthesize semantic differences between two RTL versions.”

Domain-stripped:

> “Given two transition systems, characterize their behavioral difference.”

Then search whether this is already:

* language difference,
* automata difference,
* program differencing,
* conditional equivalence,
* symbolic execution,
* counterexample classification,
* refinement,
* CEGIS,
* SyGuS,
* invariant synthesis,
* abstract interpretation,
* model checking,
* graph optimization,
* automata learning,
* CHC solving,
* program synthesis.

If yes, hardware vocabulary alone does not create novelty.

The candidate must explain why hardware structure yields a genuinely new mechanism, theorem, representation, or tractability result.

---

## TEST 2 — OUTPUT VS. MECHANISM

Ask:

> Are we inventing a new computational method, or merely producing a new artifact?

A missing:

* witness,
* contract,
* report,
* benchmark,
* IR,
* semantic diff,
* certificate,
* abstraction,

does NOT imply a new algorithm.

A new output is weak unless:

* existing methods cannot compute it,
* or computing it requires a new primitive.

---

## TEST 3 — MANUAL-BOTTLENECK FALLACY

“Humans still do this manually” proves importance.

It does NOT prove novelty.

Ask:

> Why is the human needed?

Then:

> What NEW algorithm removes that need?

If the answer is mainly:

* LLM guesses;
* hand-authored templates;
* graph matching;
* generic CEGIS;
* generic SyGuS;
* PDR;
* SAT/SMT;
* known optimization;

then the project is probably not centerpiece-level.

Lesson:

> an elegant theoretical characterization is not necessarily a new computational capability.
