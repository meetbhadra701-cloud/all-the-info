# Experiment S1 — Can open-source engines certify Yosys datapath synthesis at production widths?

Executed 2026-09-23 by Claude (Opus 5.5) inside this session. Every number below comes from a log or CSV in this folder.
Nothing here was downloaded. All tools are the pre-existing OSS CAD Suite extracted for MUXWISE Experiment 2.

## Question and kill condition

**Candidate S1.** Open-source flows lack a trustworthy way to verify Yosys arithmetic lowering (`booth`, `arith_tree`,
default `$mul`/`$macc` techmap) at production widths, and checked synthesis guidance (an open analogue of Formality SVF) would fill that gap.

- **Kill condition:** an engine already bundled with the open flow (ABC `&cec`, `cec`, `&acec`, `&polyn`) certifies
  the lowering of 16–32-bit multipliers/MACs/dot-products within 300 s, **or** a published open tool already does it.
- **Advance condition:** every bundled engine times out or is unsound on these structures, **and** no published open tool covers them.

## Designs and architectures

`rtl/` (generated by `scripts/gen.sh`):

- `mul_u`: `y = a*b` (unsigned)
- `mul_s`: `y = a*b` (signed)
- `mac_u`: `y = a*b + c`
- `dot2_u`: `y = a0*b0 + a1*b1`

Each design was built at W = 8, 16, 24, 32 with `synth -top top` (norm), `synth -booth` (booth) and `synth -arith_tree` (tree), followed by `aigmap; write_aiger -symbols -map`.
All 52 netlists synthesized. **All input/output maps matched their reference** (`MAP_MATCH`), which is a precondition for a valid miter.

## Verdict discipline

Verdicts are parsed from ABC's text (`Networks are equivalent` / `Networks are NOT EQUIVALENT`), never from exit codes.
Outer `timeout` produces TIMEOUT; anything else is ERROR or UNDECIDED.
Counterexamples were replayed on the **original** netlists with an independent AIGER evaluator (`scripts/aigsim.py`, own code), and the replayed outputs were checked against Python reference arithmetic.

## Results

### 1. Generic SAT-sweeping CEC (`&cec`) hits a wall at W = 16 (`results/cec_scaling_results.csv`)

| Pair | W=8 | W=16 | W=24 | W=32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mul_u norm vs booth | PASS 8.3 s | TIMEOUT (300 s) | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT |
| mul_s norm vs booth | – | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT |
| mac_u norm vs tree | PASS 10.0 s | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT |
| dot2_u norm vs tree | – | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT | TIMEOUT |

Controls:

- The older `cec` also proved mac_u W8 (166 s).
- Injected single-bit mutants were disproved by `&cec` in 0.02–0.2 s at the exact mutated output.

### 2. ABC's arithmetic CEC `&acec` gives spurious non-equivalence verdicts

- **mac_u W8 norm vs tree (a correct pair):** `&acec` reported `Networks are NOT EQUIVALENT` with input `32'h5CDE617B`.
  Three other checks said equivalent: `&cec` (10 s), `cec` (166 s), and a Yosys SAT miter of the tree netlist against the RTL (`SAT proof finished - no model found: SUCCESS!`).
  Replaying the counterexample under both bit orders gives **identical outputs** from both netlists, and both equal `a*b+c` (`0x8b79` / `0xfa64`).
- **mul_u W16 norm vs booth, `&acec -b`:** "Output 22 trivially differs (different phase)" at `a = b = 0`. Both original netlists output 0 there.
- **Mutants:** `&acec` "disproved" outputs 2 or 6 regardless of which bit was mutated. `&cec` disproved exactly the mutated bit.
  Replaying `&acec`'s witnesses on the originals shows they do not distinguish the circuits: `a0&b7 = 0`, so the mutation is never triggered (`results/msb_mutants_*.csv`).
- **Prediction tested and not confirmed:** in these tests, `&acec` did not give a false PASS on MSB-only mutants.
  It returned FAIL for the unrelated reason above. The theoretical risk remains (next bullet).
- **Source (`abc_source_excerpts/`):**
  - `Acec_Solve` forces the last two outputs of both normalized networks to constant 0.
  - `Acec_InsertBox` appends no auxiliary outputs, so these are real outputs.
  - `Acec_InsertBox` also zeroes secondary root literals.
  - The reported counterexample comes from the normalized miter and is not replayed on the inputs.
  - `acecCore.c` last changed substantively in January 2017. No open issue reports this behaviour (GitHub search found only #311 and #346, both about other things).

### 4. Stage-wise: the ABC step is easy; the arithmetic lowering step is the wall (`results/stagewise_w8_results.csv`)

- `&cec` pre-ABC vs post-ABC netlist (both derived from the same `synth -noabc` RTLIL): **PASS in 0.2–0.3 s for all 9 design/architecture combinations** at W=8.
- `&polyn` on the **pre-ABC** netlists:
  - mul norm and mul tree: 23–25 s, 29 M terms.
  - Every MAC and dot-product configuration, and every Booth configuration: TIMEOUT (60 s) already at W=8.
  - Yosys's pre-ABC adders come from `$alu`/`$lcu` lookahead techmap. The hypothesis that parallel-prefix carry logic causes the known backward-rewriting blow-up is **INFERRED, not tested here**.

## Interpretation

- **OBSERVED:** within the engines bundled with the standard open flow (Yosys + ABC), no engine certified Yosys arithmetic lowering at W ≥ 16 within the budget.
  - `&cec` timed out.
  - `&polyn` timed out.
  - `&acec` was unsound in the non-equivalence direction.
- **OBSERVED:** the optimization step after lowering is cheap to validate, which localizes the difficulty to lowering.
- **Prior-art result, found after the experiment and therefore not tested here:** TRACE (Kleinekathöfer, Weingarten, Datta, Drechsler, arXiv 2608.16458, Aug 17 2026, github.com/jan-kl/trace).
  - Per the paper, it verifies adders, multipliers and MACs from AIGs, including behavioral designs synthesized with Yosys/ABC.
  - Its README also lists `-dot=<n>` (dot product), `-s` (signed) and `-gen` (derive the specification polynomial) modes.
  - The same group's 2025–2026 papers cover MAC and dot-product verification.
- **Disposition: S1 is killed at the research level (occupied).** The residual is (a) integrating an algebraic checker into Yosys/EQY flows, and (b) coverage of Yosys-specific structures that TRACE has not been shown to handle: radix-4 `booth`, fused `arith_tree` trees with Baugh–Wooley constants, and `Y_WIDTH` above the natural product width.
  (b) is the only thing that could still be a research boundary; it is untested.

## Limitations

- Timeouts of 300 s (CEC), 120 s (`&polyn` scaling) and 60 s (stage-wise). One machine. No repeated runs, so timing variance was not measured.
- Only the stage-wise test at W=8 was run. Larger widths were not.
- AMulet 2, RevSCA and TRACE were **not executed**: there is no compiler in WSL, and TRACE ships only a license-less prebuilt binary, which this session will not execute.
- `&polyn` success was judged by the final monomial count matching W² (or W²+2W, 2W²) and the printed coefficient histogram. Monomials were not compared one by one.
- Scripts use absolute paths from the session scratchpad. To re-run, edit `S=` (suite path) and `OUT=` / `WK=` (work directory) at the top of each script.
